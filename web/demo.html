What you cannot see here, and why that is the point.

The plaintext. Opening the secret needs a private key that lives on a recipient's own machine and is never published, so no web page can do it for you — including this one. What is on display is the whole public surface of a shared secret: the ciphertext, who has been granted access, and the request an agent filed to release it. That surface reveals that something was shared. It does not reveal what.

The guardian approvals behind the agent's request are also absent, and deliberately: they are evaluated inside a confidential enclave. A public guardian list is a target list.
